🧠 THE SURESHOT CASE-SOLVING ALGORITHM
🟢 PHASE 1: DECONSTRUCT (The First 10% of Time)
Goal: Understand exactly what the judges want.

Step 1.1: Color-Code the Prompt (PDF 1)

Read the problem statement twice.

Highlight Yellow: The main Objective.

Highlight Blue: Constraints (budget, time, regulations).

Highlight Green: Available Data.

Highlight Red: Deliverables & Slide Limits.

Algorithm Rule: If you don't know the slide limit, STOP and find out. (PDF 2 shows limits range from 3 to 75!).

Step 1.2: Define the Core Issue

Is this a Profitability problem? (Revenue declining? Costs rising?)

Is this a Market Entry problem? (New geography or new product?)

Is this a Policy/Public Sector problem? (Stakeholder alignment needed?)

Is this an M&A problem? (Valuation, Synergies, Cultural fit?)

Algorithm Rule: Pick ONE core issue. Don't try to solve everything.

🟡 PHASE 2: THE STRATEGIC SETUP (The Next 20% of Time)
Goal: Build the skeleton before adding the meat.

Step 2.1: Form an Initial Hypothesis (PDF 1)

Write down your best guess of the answer immediately.

Example: "I hypothesize that the company is losing money because their supply chain is inefficient."

Decision Node:

If the data proves your hypothesis wrong → Falsify and pivot.

If the data proves your hypothesis right → Confirm and build your solution around it.

Step 2.2: Choose or Customize a Framework (PDF 1 & PDF 2)

Decision Node:

Does a standard framework fit perfectly? (e.g., Porter's Five Forces for a new market). → Use it.

Is the problem unique? (e.g., Tata Steel M&A or Cultural Transformation). → Customize it.

Customization Rule: Break the problem into 3-5 pillars. Give it a catchy name (e.g., the "PROB" framework, the "4C Shield"). Use the client's terminology.

Step 2.3: Build a MECE Issue Tree (PDF 1 & PDF 2)

Break your core issue into 3-4 buckets.

Test: Can you look at a data point and put it in ONLY ONE bucket? If yes, it's MECE (Mutually Exclusive). Do the buckets cover the whole problem? If yes, it's Collectively Exhaustive.

🟠 PHASE 3: RESEARCH & IDEATION (The Next 30% of Time)
Goal: Gather the evidence and form the solution.

Step 3.1: The 5-Layer Research Dive (PDF 1 & PDF 2)

Use the Research Toolkit (Gartner, McKinsey, PwC, EY, ResearchGate).

Layer 1: Company (Financials, current strategy).

Layer 2: Industry (Market size, CAGR, trends).

Layer 3: Customer (Segmentation, behavior, pain points).

Layer 4: Competitors (Benchmarking, market share).

Layer 5: Misc (Regulations, ESG, Tech disruption).

Algorithm Rule: Triangulate. Never rely on a single data point. Cite your sources.

Step 3.2: Ideate & Prioritize (PDF 1)

Brainstorm individually first (avoid groupthink).

Filter ideas using the F.I.A. Test:

Feasibility (Can it be done?)

Impact (Will it move the needle?)

Alignment (Does it answer the prompt?)

Step 3.3: Backward Induction (The "Pre-Mortem") (PDF 1)

Imagine your solution failed. Why did it fail?

Identify those failure points and build mitigation strategies into your plan (e.g., "We recommend blockchain, BUT we need a contingency fund for transition costs").

🔴 PHASE 4: THE BUILD (The Next 20% of Time)
Goal: Create a visually clean, top-down presentation.

Step 4.1: The SCQA Storyline (PDF 1 & PDF 2)

Slide 1 (Executive Summary):

Situation (Context)

Complication (The Problem)

Question (What should we do?)

Answer (Your Recommendation)

Step 4.2: Top-Down Slide Design (PDF 1)

Rule of Action Titles: Write the "so what" as the slide title. (Not "Financial Analysis" → But "Cost reduction of 15% can be achieved by automating supply chain").

Slide Economy: Every slide must have a purpose. If it doesn't support the storyline, cut it.

Visuals: Use Flaticons or Iconscout for icons. Use Slide Geeks or Slidesgo for templates. Keep fonts and colors consistent.

Step 4.3: The Financials & Appendix (PDF 1 & PDF 2)

Tie your recommendation to ROI, NPV, or Cost Savings.

Put detailed models, assumptions, and alternative ideas in the Appendix.

Algorithm Rule: Do not put your DCF model on Slide 3. Put the insight from the DCF on Slide 3, and the model in the Appendix.

🟣 PHASE 5: DELIVERY & DEFENSE (The Final 20% of Time)
Goal: Nail the presentation and the Q&A.

Step 5.1: Rehearse the Transitions

Do not just memorize your own part. Know exactly how you are handing off to your teammate.

Step 5.2: The Q&A Protocol (PDF 1)

Listen fully: Do not interrupt the judge.

Maintain composure: Even if the question is harsh.

Link back: Answer the question, then tie it back to your core recommendation.

Teamwork: If the question is about Financials, and your teammate built the model, smoothly pass it: "My teammate built the DCF; I'll let them answer the specifics of the discount rate."

Step 5.3: Post-Event Networking (PDF 2)

Connect with the judges and the winning teams on LinkedIn (as listed in the Acknowledgements).

🚨 THE "SURE-SHOT" DECISION TREE (If you get stuck)
If you are in the middle of a case and feel lost, run this check:

Are we answering the actual prompt? (Re-read the color-coded prompt).

Is our analysis MECE? (Are there overlaps or gaps?).

Is our solution backed by numbers? (If not, go back to research).

Is our slide too text-heavy? (If yes, convert words to a visual framework).

Are we being too theoretical? (Look at PDF 2; judges want implementation roadmaps, risk mitigation, and ROI).
